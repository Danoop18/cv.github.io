### Scientific Question

How do reactive nitrogen and oxygen species (specifically peroxynitrite dynamics) modulate cellular signaling, tissue remodeling, and positional memory during limb regeneration in the axolotl (*Ambystoma mexicanum*)?

### Context

The axolotl is a key model organism in regenerative biology due to its extraordinary capacity to regrow complex biological structures, including limbs, cardiac tissue, and central nervous system components, without scarring. Understanding the early redox signaling cascades that trigger blastema formation is essential for deciphering fundamental cellular plasticity and tissue repair mechanisms. This research was conducted as part of M.Sc. studies at CINVESTAV-UGA (Irapuato, Mexico).

### Experimental Approach

- **Molecular Biology & Sample Preparation**: Optimization of tissue harvesting, fixation, and molecular labeling protocols across early and late regeneration stages.
- **Advanced Microscopy**: Utilization of confocal laser scanning microscopy to capture high-resolution spatio-temporal dynamics of reactive species in regenerating tissue.
- **Image Analysis & Data Structuring**: Application of quantitative image analysis and Python workflows to extract signal intensity, spatial distribution, and cellular localization metrics.

### What was investigated

- Spatio-temporal distribution of peroxynitrite accumulation during early post-amputation wound healing and blastema formation.
- Cellular localization patterns of oxidative stress markers in blastema cells versus non-regenerating control tissues.
- Methodological refinement of fluorescence imaging protocols for live and fixed urodele amphibian tissues.
