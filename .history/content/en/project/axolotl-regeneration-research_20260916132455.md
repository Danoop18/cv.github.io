### Scientific Question

How do reactive nitrogen and oxygen species (specifically peroxynitrite dynamics) modulate cell signaling cascades, tissue remodeling, and positional memory during limb regeneration in the axolotl (*Ambystoma mexicanum*)?

{{< img src="uploads/ajolote_research.png" alt="Banner: Tissue regeneration research" >}}

### Context

The axolotl is a fundamental model organism in regenerative biology due to its extraordinary ability to regenerate complex biological structures (limbs, cardiac tissue, and central nervous system components) without scarring. Understanding the early redox signaling cascades that trigger blastema formation is essential for deciphering cellular plasticity and tissue repair. This research was conducted as part of a Master's degree program at CINVESTAV-UGA (Irapuato, Mexico).

### Experimental Approach

- **Molecular Biology and Sample Preparation**: Optimization of tissue collection and fixation protocols; molecular labeling using fluorescent probes at early and late post-amputation stages; design of mounting strategies and confocal analysis protocols.
- **Advanced Microscopy**: Use of laser scanning confocal microscopy to capture the spatiotemporal dynamics of labeled reactive species in real-time within the tissue during the initial stages of regeneration.
- **Image and Data Analysis**: Application of semi-quantitative analysis to extract metrics regarding signal intensity, spatial distribution, and cellular localization throughout the early phases of regeneration.

### Research Focus

- Spatiotemporal distribution of peroxynitrite, superoxide, and nitric oxide accumulation during the early phases of wound healing and blastema formation post-amputation.
- Cellular localization patterns of oxidative stress markers in blastemal cells versus non-regenerative control tissues.
- Methodological refinement of mounting, probing, and fluorescence protocols for amphibian tissues. ### Current Status
